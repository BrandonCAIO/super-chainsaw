"Icons are remembered.
Legends are felt."

In 1970s São Paulo, Percival Lafer was building something the European modernists hadn't considered: furniture shaped by the body's desire to rest, not by geometry's desire to impress. He used the rosewood and hardwoods of Brazil's interior — materials with warmth that Scandinavian pine never had — and wrapped them in leather the color of a late afternoon in Ipanema.

Fifty years later, a pair of his MP-97 chairs sits waiting. The leather has been carefully restored. The frames have not moved. The original label is still there. Nothing has been reinvented because nothing needed to be.
